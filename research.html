Research

My research program, decision-making for cyber-physical systems, studies systems in which physical processes interact with data acquisition, computation, and control. I approach them from the systems view of industrial engineering. Its central question is: How can we build technically robust, economically viable, and computationally tractable decisions and policies for cyber-physical systems? It covers energy networks, water networks, and production and logistics systems, and combines six families of methods: exact mathematical optimization, optimization under uncertainty, sequential decision-making, approximate optimization, statistical learning, and network science.

Learning system behavior from operational data

How to obtain, from heterogeneous observations and records of varying quality and length, representations that are useful for diagnosis and decision-making. This line covers forecasting, fault detection, synthetic data generation, and representation learning, with applications in photovoltaic generation, water demand, maintenance of industrial assets, and robotics.

Statistical Learning Forecasting Fault Detection Synthetic Data

Prescriptive decision-making under uncertainty

Formulations and algorithms for investment, resource allocation, and operation decisions. The guiding question is how to represent physical and operational constraints in enough detail, include the relevant uncertainty, and obtain solutions within the available time. Applications include distribution network planning and reconfiguration, microgrid energy management, vehicle routing, container loading, and airline seat assignment.

Mathematical Programming Stochastic Optimization Heuristics Reinforcement Learning

Coordination of distributed decisions

How the decisions of multiple agents with partial information and shared resources relate to one another: what information they need to coordinate, how interaction rules shape their policies, and which trade-offs arise between individual performance, collective outcome, and the operation of the infrastructure. Local energy markets are the main application, as in MARLEM, a multi-agent reinforcement learning simulation framework for implicit cooperation in decentralized local energy markets.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Local Energy Markets Implicit Cooperation

Characterization of network structure

The relationship between the structure of a network and its ability to sustain service under disturbances. The guiding question is which connectivity properties identify vulnerable components and configurations, and how they should be combined with the representation of flows and operation. This is my most recent line; it uses spectral graph theory, topological analysis, and graph neural networks, with emphasis on water and power distribution networks.

Network Science Spectral Graph Theory Graph Neural Networks Resilience

Spaces for experimentation

The Solar Living Lab, a living laboratory for microgrids on the top floor of the Mario Laserna building, brings together four departments of the School of Engineering and supports the instrumentation, monitoring, and validation of methods with real data. The Department's Integrated Learning Environment (AIA), which I have coordinated since 2026, hosts learning and research activities in Industry 4.0, with collaborative robotics and digital twins.

Microgrids IoT Collaborative Robotics Digital Twins

Current priorities

My research priorities are the two newest lines, coordination of distributed decisions and characterization of network structure; the Hardware-in-the-Loop validation of MARLEM and a peer-to-peer market pilot on the campus microgrids; and the graduation of my three Ph.D. candidates. In the longer term and subject to funding, I aim to turn the AIA into an artificial intelligence laboratory for cyber-physical systems, in collaboration with the departments closest to that purpose.

Cyber-Physical Systems Hardware-in-the-Loop Artificial Intelligence
